Being reachable

So the person you invite can actually answer.

Nobody may reach you in Sereus Chat without an invitation — that is the whole point. But right now nobody can reach you either, and that is a different problem. A phone in your pocket has no address the world can knock on.

There are two ways to fix it. Neither is the obvious answer; they suit different people.

Borrow a relay

A relay is somebody's always-on machine that passes traffic along so people can reach you. It is the quick way to get started, and you can stop using it whenever you like.

What it costs you. A relay carries your traffic sealed and cannot read anything you say. But whoever runs it can see that you are talking to someone, when, and roughly how much. You are choosing a particular person to know that about you. Pick one you are willing to trust with that much — and change it later if you want to.

Open relays

  • sereus.org community relay — operated by the Sereus project. Free, no sign-up, a limited number of slots.
    Use this relay Not running yet — listed here ahead of the relay itself.

Tapping a relay does not switch anything on by itself. Your app will show you who runs it and what they would be able to see, and you decide there.

Run your own

Nothing borrowed, nobody else in it. This is the answer most people want in the end, and the only one where no stranger sees the shape of your conversations.

  1. Run a relay on a machine that stays on — a spare computer, or a small server you rent.
  2. Point your app at it the same way as any other relay.
  3. Offer it to friends if you like. One relay can serve more than one person.

A relay is a small program. It holds none of your messages and joins none of your conversations.

How, exactly. The relay quickstart has the step-by-step (Docker, a DNS name, a few commands). It is written for the technically inclined — if that is not you, it is a fine thing to hand to whoever in your life runs servers.

Going further. A relay only makes you reachable. If you want an always-on machine that also keeps your own copy of your conversations — a full cadre node — start with the cadre node guide.

Or host for other people. If you have a machine that is always on and friends who are getting started, cadre-host runs nodes that join their cadres — they keep control of their own conversations, you contribute the uptime.

Paying somebody to do it

Some people would rather rent this than run it. Providers who offer a machine you control will be listed here as they appear. That is not the same as borrowing somebody else's: on a machine you control, no stranger sees the shape of your conversations.

What a relay is not

  • It is not an account. Nothing to sign up for, no password.
  • It does not hold your messages. Those live with you and the people you talk to.
  • It does not decide who may reach you. Only your invitations do that.
  • It can go away. If yours stops working your conversations are unaffected — you simply stop being reachable by anyone new until you pick another.
